ABCDE Patient Assessment Student Nurse Pocket Guide • Adult Critical Care ASSESS → RECOGNISE → ESCALATE → REASSESS Start with the immediate threat. Look at the patient, not just the monitor. Consider baseline, trends and the support the patient needs. Student nurse role: recognise change, assess systematically, monitor, document, communicate clearly and escalate within your competence. A — Airway Assess Is the airway patent? Can the patient speak and protect their airway? Secretions, vomit or swelling? Listen for snoring, gurgling or stridor. If ETT/tracheostomy: secure, connected, functioning and documented position. Red flags Stridor • gurgling • choking • reduced consciousness • copious secretions • blocked/displaced airway device Ventilated patient suddenly deteriorates: think DOPE — Displacement, Obstruction, Pneumothorax, Equipment failure — and escalate urgently. B — Breathing Assess Respiratory rate and trend SpO₂ and oxygen requirement Work of breathing / accessory muscles Chest expansion and symmetry Breath sounds Oxygen/HFNO/NIV/ventilator support and alarms RR 12–20/min Common adult reference SpO₂ 94–98% Usually for most acutely ill adults SpO₂ 88–92% Common target if at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure Red flags Falling SpO₂ • increasing oxygen requirement • marked tachypnoea/bradypnoea • exhaustion • cyanosis • severe work of breathing • unequal chest movement • markedly reduced breath sounds C — Circulation Assess HR, rhythm and trend BP/MAP in clinical context CRT, skin colour/temperature, mottling Peripheral pulses Bleeding/fluid loss Fluid balance and urine output IV, arterial and central access: site, security and function HR 60–100 bpm Common adult resting reference Urine ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr Common adult reference CRT <2 sec Common reference; interpret in context BP/MAP Patient-specific targets matter Red flags Hypotension • tachycardia • prolonged CRT • cool/mottled skin • weak pulses • falling urine output • active bleeding • new altered consciousness D — Disability Assess Change from baseline ACVPU: Alert, new Confusion, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive GCS where indicated Pupils: size, equality and reaction Blood glucose Pain, sedation and delirium GCS 15/15 Maximum score; assess components when required Glucose <4.0 mmol/L Common hypoglycaemia threshold Red flags New confusion • reduced responsiveness • seizure • new unequal/poorly reactive pupils • rapidly changing neurological status Remember: New confusion may be secondary to problems elsewhere in ABCDE. Reassess the whole patient. E — Exposure Assess Temperature and trend Skin, wounds and surgical sites Bleeding or fluid loss Abdomen where appropriate Pressure areas and device-related pressure Drains, dressings and output Swelling/oedema, limb changes and overall appearance General adult temperature reference: approximately 36–37.5°C. Always consider baseline, trend and clinical context. After ABCDE What has changed? Is the patient getting worse? What support are they requiring? Is that support increasing? What needs escalating now? What will you reassess? Reassess after intervention. ABCDE is a cycle, not a one-time checklist. Escalate with SBAR S — Situation: What is happening now? B — Background: Relevant diagnosis, procedure, history and treatment. A — Assessment: What have you found? Give important observations and trends. R — Recommendation: What are you concerned about and what help do you need? Key reminder Do not just ask “Is the number normal?” Ask: “What was it before? What support is the patient receiving? Is the trend changing? Does the patient look worse?” Educational resource: Use alongside local NHS Trust policies, escalation pathways, oxygen targets, observation charts and clinical supervision. This guide does not replace local policy or professional judgement.
